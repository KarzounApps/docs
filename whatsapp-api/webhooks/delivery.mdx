---
title: "Delivery and retries"
description: "How Karzoun delivers, retries and logs WhatsApp webhook events."
---

## Success

A delivery succeeds when your endpoint answers with any `2xx` status within **10 seconds**. Redirects (`3xx`) are not followed and count as failures.

## Retries

A failed delivery is retried on this schedule:

| Attempt | After the previous failure |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | 10 seconds |
| 3 | 1 minute |
| 4 | 5 minutes |
| 5 | 30 minutes |
| 6 | 2 hours |
| 7 | 6 hours |
| 8 | 12 hours |

After 8 attempts (about 21 hours) the event is marked **failed**. Retries survive restarts on Karzoun's side.

If an endpoint fails 20 times in a row, Karzoun pauses deliveries to it for 5 minutes, then tries again. Events keep queueing during the pause, and the pause doesn't use up attempts.

## Delivery log

Open the number's **API** tab and click **View deliveries** in the **Webhook URL** card. Each event of the last 30 days shows:

| Column | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| Event | Message (with its type), status (with its value) or the account-level field |
| Status | **Delivered**, **Retrying** (with the next try) or **Failed** |
| Response | Your endpoint's HTTP status, the response time, and the first 300 characters of an error response |

## Resend

Click **Resend** on a failed event to send it again, with a fresh round of retries. It keeps its `X-Karzoun-Event-Id`, so your deduplication still works. Failed events can be resent for 30 days.

Events that happen while a number has no webhook URL are not kept, so they can't be sent later.

## Design a stable endpoint

- Return `200` before doing any work. Put the event on a queue and process it separately.
- Expect bursts: a campaign of 1,000 messages produces up to about 3,000 status events.
- Keep the endpoint's median response under 250 ms.
- Make processing idempotent on `X-Karzoun-Event-Id`.
