---
title: "Opt-in and opt-out"
description: "Get permission before messaging people on WhatsApp, and let them stop messages easily."
---

You may start a conversation with someone on WhatsApp only if:

- they gave you their phone number, **and**
- they agreed to receive WhatsApp messages from your business.

Collecting this permission (opt-in) is your responsibility, and it must follow WhatsApp's Business Messaging Policy and the laws that apply to you. A message started without opt-in is the fastest way to get blocked, lose [quality](/whatsapp-api/account/quality-rating) and get your account restricted.

## Requirements

When you collect opt-in:

- Say clearly that the person agrees to receive messages **on WhatsApp**.
- Say clearly **which business** will message them.
- Follow the laws that apply to you, such as consent and data protection rules.

## Where to collect it

Any channel works, as long as the consent is clear:

| Channel | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Website or app | A checkbox at sign-up or checkout: "Send me order updates on WhatsApp" |
| WhatsApp itself | The user messages you first, for example from a [QR code or link](/whatsapp-api/guides/bring-users-to-whatsapp), and you ask whether they want updates |
| SMS or email | A message with a link or keyword to confirm |
| Phone or IVR | Consent given during a call |
| In store or on paper | A signed form or a QR code at the counter |

A person messaging you first counts as opt-in for replying to that conversation. It doesn't mean they agreed to receive marketing later. Ask.

## Best practice

- **Be specific.** Separate opt-ins for order updates and for offers. People who only wanted shipping updates block businesses that send promotions.
- **Set expectations.** Say what you'll send and how often.
- **Store proof.** Keep when and where each person opted in, and for what.
- **Send something soon.** A welcome message right after opt-in reminds people who you are.
- **Don't buy lists.** Numbers from third parties aren't opt-in.

## Opt-out

People must be able to stop your messages easily, and you must respect it.

- Add a **"Stop promotions"** quick-reply button to marketing templates. When it's tapped you get a [`button` event](/whatsapp-api/webhooks/events#template-button) with your payload: remove the person from marketing lists right away.
- Recognize replies such as "STOP", "إيقاف" or "unsubscribe", and confirm the opt-out.
- Keep sending transactional messages they still need, such as an order they placed, unless they ask you to stop those too.

### Marketing preference in WhatsApp

Users can also stop marketing messages from a business directly in WhatsApp. When they do:

- your webhook receives a `user_preferences` event,
- marketing templates to them fail with error `131050`,
- utility, authentication and service messages still go through.

When the user turns marketing back on, you receive another `user_preferences` event.
